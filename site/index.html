"One wrong digit and the money's gone. Now what?"

The problem

What happens today

When a user sends money to the wrong UPI ID, there is no undo. They have to deal with the recipient, their bank, the recipient's bank, NPCI and sometimes the RBI Ombudsman, with little idea who owns the case or what comes next.

Money can only come back if the recipient agrees, so every day of delay makes recovery less likely.

Goals

  • Faster recovery initiation
  • A transparent, trackable process
  • More wrong payments actually recovered

Non-goals

  • Guaranteeing every reversal
  • Reversing payments directly
  • Preventing every accidental payment
North Star
% of eligible wrong-UPI cases recovered

Who it's for

Two personas with the same problem: Ritika, a metro salaried user, and Sunita, a tier-2 shop owner who needs Hindi, a person to call and a flow that works on slow internet.

India today

Every app shows the payee's bank name before paying, and routes complaints through NPCI's UDIR. But the dispute is a generic form, sometimes locked for 2 days, and the recipient returning the money stays voluntary.

Brazil (Pix)

The recipient gets a "Return payment" button, a one-tap full or partial return within 90 days. A contest button makes the receiving bank freeze the funds.

Australia

Report a mistaken payment within 10 business days and the money comes back, even without the recipient agreeing, if it's still there. Speed is everything.

The gap: no Indian app has a recovery flow built for wrong-payee mistakes, with an owner, a deadline and an easy way for the recipient to return the money.

The solution

1

One-tap Recover

A "Recover payment" button on every UPI receipt that sends a pre-filled recovery request in seconds, so users report while the money can still be traced.

2

Easy Return

The recipient gets a clear request with a one-tap "Return payment" button, so returning a wrong payment is easier than ignoring it.

3

Live Tracker

A plain-language timeline showing who has the case, what happens next and by when, so users can see progress instead of chasing support.

Requirements

Functional

Users can raise a recovery request in one tap from any UPI receipt, with the payment details filled in automatically. The recipient gets a one-tap option to return the money, and both sides can follow the case on a live tracker from start to close.

P

Performance: request sent within 10 seconds; tracker updates within 1 minute of a bank update; works on 3G.

Q

Quality: requests auto-filled and eligibility-checked; under 2% sent back for errors.

R

Reliability: one case per payment; no request lost if the app or network drops.

S

Secure: encrypted, stored in India, DPDP-compliant; returns need a UPI PIN and a real payment.

T

Transparency: every case shows owner, next step, deadline and final outcome.

U

Usability: 3 taps or fewer, no jargon, available in 5+ Indian languages.

What the review caught before any code

A privacy leak

A return payment would have shown the sender's UPI ID, which often contains a phone number. Fixed by showing a masked name ("Ritika S."), with a spike planned to prove it can be built.

Two stories that contradicted each other

One story hid the Recover button on ineligible payments, while another needed users to tap it. Fixed with a "Help → I paid the wrong person" entry.

A quarter-long story

"Prefilled request + case ID" was sized XL. Splitting it into a screen (M) and a case service (L) exposed the critical path, and the plan went from 5 to 9 weeks.

The scam path

A scam report must never contact the recipient. It goes straight to the 1930 helpline and cybercrime.gov.in, so scammers can't use the flow to pressure people.
